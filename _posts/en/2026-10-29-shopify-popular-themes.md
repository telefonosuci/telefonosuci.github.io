When choosing a theme it is natural to look at what everyone else uses. It makes sense: a widespread theme has more developers who know it, more compatible apps and more answers in the forums. But "most used" does not mean "best for you", and the rankings that circulate online measure different things.

This article is for people who run a Shopify store and are choosing (or changing) a theme. It reviews the most widespread themes, the reasons merchants like them and the reasons they do not, and explains where to find the rankings and how to read them. If sections, blocks and theme blocks are not yet clear, start with the [guide to Shopify themes](/en/blog/shopify-themes-merchant/).

### First: how to read the rankings

No ranking comes from Shopify. They are all estimates by external services that visit millions of stores and read the theme name in the page code. That is why:

- **they do not agree**: depending on the source, first place goes to Dawn or to a paid theme, and percentages can differ by a factor of two;
- **free themes are over-represented**: every new store starts with the default theme, and many test or abandoned stores never change it. That is partly why Dawn is at the top;
- **renamed or customised themes slip through**: if an agency built a theme on Dawn and named it after the brand, it is sometimes counted as Dawn, sometimes not;
- **they count stores, not sales**: a theme used by 10,000 tiny stores beats one used by 2,000 brands turning over millions.

Use the rankings to see **which themes are mature and supported**, not to decide on their own.

### Shopify's free themes

#### Dawn

Released in 2021 with Online Store 2.0, it was the default theme for new stores for four years and is still the most widespread in almost every ranking (around 15% of detected stores).[^detector] The other free themes of that generation derive from Dawn too: Refresh, Sense, Craft, Studio, Crave, Ride, Taste, Origin, Colorblock, Spotlight, Publisher.

**Why merchants like it**

- it is free and maintained by Shopify;
- it is fast: little JavaScript, good Core Web Vitals scores;
- every Shopify developer knows it, and almost every app is tested on Dawn;
- it is a clean base for anyone who wants custom sections built.

**Why not**

- it is deliberately minimal: no rich mega menus, advanced filters, lookbooks or bundles;
- it is recognisable: many stores look the same;
- it is built on "classic" sections, less flexible than the theme blocks of newer themes;
- Shopify has moved its focus to Horizon: Dawn still gets updates, but new features land elsewhere first.

#### Horizon and its family

Introduced in May 2025, it is the new default theme: a single codebase that produces ten themes with different styles (Horizon, Atelier, Dwell, Fabric, Heritage, Pitch, Ritual, Savor, Tinker, Vessel). In just over a year it has climbed to second place in several rankings.[^detector] The full comparison is in [Dawn and Horizon](/en/blog/dawn-horizon/).

**Why merchants like it**

- it is built on **theme blocks**: you can nest groups, columns and blocks and compose pages that needed a developer with Dawn;
- it is free and is the first to get Shopify's new features, including those tied to the editor and AI;
- it looks more modern than Dawn and comes with ten starting styles.

**Why not**

- more freedom also means more ways to build messy, heavy pages;
- it is young: some apps do not yet have blocks designed for it, and fewer developers know it well;
- in real-world speed measurements it is still slightly behind Dawn.[^commercerank]

#### Debut and the other "vintage" themes

Debut was the default theme before 2021 and still ranks high in some lists, because many stores opened in those years never changed it.[^commercerank] It is not a theme to choose today: it predates Online Store 2.0 (sections on the home page only, no app blocks) and is no longer developed. If your store still runs it, moving to a 2.0 theme is one of the investments with the clearest return.

### The most widespread paid themes

Paid Theme Store themes are a one-off purchase, usually between 300 and 400 dollars, with updates included. The ones you meet most often:

| Theme | Developer | Built for | Why merchants like it | Why not |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Prestige** | Maestrooo | Fashion, luxury, brands with polished photography | Editorial, elegant look with great attention to detail | Only works with high-end photos; not suited to large, promotional catalogues |
| **Impact** | Maestrooo | Brands with a strong identity, launches, DTC | Large typography, animations, rich product pages | Easy to make heavy; the "loud" style does not suit everyone |
| **Warehouse** | Maestrooo | Large catalogues, electronics, spare parts, B2B | Search, filters and navigation designed for thousands of products | More functional than emotional |
| **Impulse** | Archetype Themes | Fashion, stores with many promotions | Very complete (quick view, promotions, slideshows), among the fastest in its class[^detector] | Many options = learning curve; you see it everywhere |
| **Motion** | Archetype Themes | Brands built on video and imagery | Video, parallax and animations with no development | Animations cost speed, especially on mobile |
| **Symmetry** | Clean Canvas | Medium and large catalogues | Very solid mega menus and collection organisation | Less distinctive design |
| **Empire** | Pixel Union | Very large catalogues, "department store" style | Navigation and search for thousands of products | Looks dated without good design work |

Some rankings also put lesser-known paid themes at the top (for example **Trademark**), chosen mostly by established stores with large catalogues.[^koala] It is a good reminder: the ranking depends heavily on which stores the service manages to detect.

### Why a theme is (really) liked

Beyond the names, the themes that stay at the top year after year almost always share the same qualities:

- **they are updated often**: they follow Shopify's new features instead of falling behind;
- **the developer answers**: fast support, clear documentation, a public changelog;
- **they work with the most used apps**, through app blocks and without code changes;
- **they hold up with the real catalogue**, not just the demo one;
- **they are fast for real customers**, not just in the demo's PageSpeed score.

### Why a theme disappoints

The reasons merchants switch theme after a year are just as recurring:

- **it was chosen from the demo**: with the store's own photos, real copy and 400 variants it no longer looks like that;
- **it has too many features**: you pay in speed for what you do not use, and the editor becomes hard to use;
- **its code was edited** to the point where it can no longer be updated (covered in the [theme guide](/en/blog/shopify-themes-merchant/));
- **everyone in your sector uses it**: the store does not stand out from competitors;
- **the developer stopped updating it**: the most serious warning sign, because sooner or later something breaks.

### Where to find the rankings

| Source | What it measures | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [Store Leads](https://storeleads.app/reports/shopify) | Theme share across active Shopify stores, filterable by country and category | Free public report; detailed data is paid |
| [BuiltWith](https://trends.builtwith.com/shop/shopify-theme) | Theme distribution, also by country and traffic band | The view on the most visited sites is useful; some pages are not updated often |
| [Shopify Theme Detector](https://shopthemedetector.com/shopify-themes) | Ranking of detected themes, with a page per theme | Also tells you which theme a specific store uses |
| [CommerceRank](https://www.commercerank.ai/insights/state-of-shopify-themes-2026) | "State of Shopify Themes": adoption and average speed per theme | Periodic report on a sample of stores |
| [Shopify – Theme performance](https://performance.shopify.com/pages/theme-performance-data-table) | Real Core Web Vitals per theme, Shopify data | Not a popularity ranking, but the most reliable source on **speed** |
| [Theme Vitals](https://buttondown.com/theme-vitals) | Monthly newsletter on theme Core Web Vitals | Useful to see which themes improve or worsen over time |
| [Shopify Theme Store](https://themes.shopify.com/) | Merchant reviews and ratings | Read recent reviews, not just the average score |

Two practical tips:

- **cross-check at least two sources** and look at the trend, not the exact number;
- **for speed, trust real-user data** (Shopify's table, Theme Vitals) over tests on the demo.

### How to use this information

1. **Start from your catalogue and budget**, not the ranking: how many products, how many variants, how much editorial look matters compared to search.
2. **Make a shortlist of 2-3 mature themes** (in the rankings for years, recently updated).
3. **Check real-world speed** in Shopify's table.
4. **Preview the themes with your own products**: it is free with no time limit, you pay only when you publish.
5. **If none convinces you 100%**, consider a free theme (Horizon) plus a few custom sections: it often costs less than a paid theme bent out of shape.

### In short

- **Dawn** is still the most widespread, **Horizon** is the new standard and growing fast; **Debut** is in the rankings only out of inertia.
- Among paid themes, **Prestige, Impact, Warehouse** (Maestrooo), **Impulse, Motion** (Archetype), **Symmetry** and **Empire** recur: each has a precise audience.
- A theme is liked when it is **updated, supported, fast for real customers** and holds up with the **real catalogue**.
- The rankings (Store Leads, BuiltWith, Shopify Theme Detector, CommerceRank) **do not agree**: use them to see which themes are mature, not to choose for you.

**Shopify** is a subscription-based (SaaS) ecommerce platform. Hosting, security, payments, checkout, updates and PCI compliance are handled by Shopify[^pci]: the merchant takes care of products, marketing and customers. It's one of the most widely used platforms in the world, from small shops to large brands (on **Shopify Plus**).

### How it works

A Shopify store has three layers:

- **Admin**: the dashboard for products, variants, inventory, orders, customers, discounts and shipping.
- **Theme (Online Store)**: the actual website, written in **Liquid** (Shopify's template language) and organised into *sections* and *blocks* that the merchant edits in the visual editor without touching code.
- **Checkout**: run by Shopify, optimised and structurally the same for everyone. It's customised with *Checkout UI Extensions* and *Shopify Functions* (custom discounts, payment and shipping methods).

Around it there are the **APIs** (Admin API and Storefront API, in GraphQL), **webhooks** to react to events (order created, product updated) and the **App Store** with thousands of apps.

### Typical use cases

- **D2C brands** (fashion, beauty, food, design) that want a polished store, fast.
- **Multichannel**: the same catalogue sold on the website, Instagram, Facebook, Google, TikTok, marketplaces and physical stores with **Shopify POS**, with a single inventory.
- **International selling**: with **Shopify Markets** you manage currencies, languages, domains and per-country pricing from one store.
- **B2B**: dedicated price lists, payment terms and wholesale ordering (native on Shopify Plus).
- **Integration with business systems**: syncing orders, stock and customer data with ERPs (SAP, Microsoft Dynamics, Odoo), PIMs and CRMs via APIs and webhooks.
- **Headless**: Shopify as the catalogue and checkout engine, with a separate frontend built in **Hydrogen** (Shopify's React framework) or Next.js.

### Advantages

- **Time to market**: a professional store launches in weeks, not months.
- **Zero infrastructure**: no servers, security patches or traffic spikes to manage. Black Friday is Shopify's problem, not yours.
- **One of the best-converting checkouts** on the market, with **Shop Pay** speeding up payment for returning customers.
- **A huge ecosystem**: apps for reviews, subscriptions, loyalty, email marketing, translations, logistics.
- **Reliable development**: theme changes are prepared and tested on a copy before going live, and the integration APIs are solid and well documented.
- **Easy for merchants**: the admin is intuitive and the marketing team becomes self-sufficient.

### Disadvantages

- **Recurring costs**: a monthly subscription, transaction fees if you don't use Shopify Payments, and paid apps that add up over time.
- **Limited customisation in some areas**: the checkout and some structures (for example the URL format of product pages) can't be changed freely.
- **Data limits**: a maximum number of options and variants per product[^options]; very complex catalogues need metafields, metaobjects or apps.
- **Platform lock-in**: moving elsewhere is possible but takes work.
- **Apps that slow you down**: each app can inject scripts into the theme, and too many hurt performance.

### Comparison with alternatives

| | Shopify | WooCommerce | Magento / Adobe Commerce | PrestaShop | BigCommerce |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model | SaaS | Open source WordPress plugin | Open source / enterprise licence | Open source | SaaS |
| Hosting | Included | Your responsibility | Your responsibility (or Adobe cloud) | Your responsibility | Included |
| Speed to launch | Very high | Medium | Low | Medium | High |
| Customisation | High, within limits | Total | Total | High | High, within limits |
| Maintenance | Minimal | Updates, security, plugins | Heavy, needs a team | Medium | Minimal |
| Best for | Growing D2C brands up to enterprise | Content sites with a shop | Very complex catalogues and rules | European SMBs | Large catalogues and B2B |

- **Shopify vs WooCommerce**: WooCommerce is free and fully modifiable, but hosting, security, performance and plugin compatibility are on you. Shopify costs more each month but much less in maintenance.
- **Shopify vs Magento**: Magento handles extremely complex logic (multi-store, huge catalogues, intricate pricing rules) but requires specialised developers and dedicated infrastructure. Many brands move from Magento to Shopify Plus precisely to cut running costs.
- **Shopify vs PrestaShop**: PrestaShop is popular in Italy and France and has no licence fee, but its module ecosystem is more fragmented and maintenance is on you.

### When to choose it (and when not)

**Yes**, if you want to sell online reliably without running infrastructure, if your value lies in product and marketing, and if you want a self-sufficient marketing team.

**No**, or not on its own, if your sales model needs a radically different checkout, very unusual product logic (complex configurators with thousands of combinations, prices computed in real time by external systems), or if the budget can't absorb recurring costs. In those cases consider headless Shopify, or an open source platform.

[^pci]: Shopify, [PCI DSS compliance](https://www.shopify.com/pci-compliant).
[^options]: Shopify Help Center, [Adding variants](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/variants/add-variants): up to 3 options per product.
